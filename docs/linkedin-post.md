A rare disease family shouldn't have to become a research team just to learn who has already studied something like their child's condition.

At the Hack-Nation 7th Global AI Hackathon, we built **RarePath Atlas** for the Buffalo Initiative × OpenAI challenge: an atlas that connects rare-disease research, studies and patient communities, with evidence behind every connection.

Our test case is CDKL5 deficiency disorder. It was once labelled a Rett syndrome variant, but it's now recognised as its own disorder. Even so, people with CDD were enrolled in the same NIH natural-history study as Rett, and that shared work later informed a CDD-specific severity scale.

RarePath surfaces that precedent in one search. It also shows what it doesn't mean: shared infrastructure isn't shared biology, and nothing suggests treatments carry over.

Every claim opens to its exact source quote. OpenAI extracts and explains the evidence; it never replaces it.

Rare shouldn't mean researching alone.
