# The 10× case: one milestone, measured honestly

## The milestone

**Identifying and evaluating an existing research asset or collaboration precedent.**

This is an early step on the road to treatment. Before a patient organization can run a natural-history study, design a trial-readiness program or fund an outcome-measure project, it needs to know what already exists, who built it, and whether any of it applies.

We do **not** claim RarePath Atlas speeds up treatment development as a whole. That would not be defensible. The claim is narrower and testable.

## The claim

> RarePath compresses the discovery and evidence-assembly portion of this milestone from many disconnected research steps into one evidence-backed workflow.

## Traditional path

1. Search PubMed manually
2. Search disease registries
3. Search ClinicalTrials.gov
4. Identify adjacent disease communities
5. Work out whether shared infrastructure existed
6. Contact researchers
7. Compare protocols and outcome measures
8. Decide whether anything is reusable

Each step uses a different system with its own vocabulary, and several need expert interpretation. In the CDD example, the key fact (CDD participants were enrolled in NCT02738281) sits in a registry eligibility field and a paper's methods section, not in any abstract headline.

## RarePath path

1. Search the disease
2. See evidence-backed adjacent research history
3. Discover the shared study and infrastructure
4. Inspect exactly why each connection exists (verbatim quotes)
5. See known differences and limitations
6. Identify the reusable or adaptable research asset
7. Leave with a concrete validation question

## What the CDD demo actually assembles

These counts describe the dataset; they are not time estimates.

- **7 source systems:** PubMed, PubMed Central, ClinicalTrials.gov, NIH RePORTER, MONDO/HPO via EBI OLS, HGNC, and official organization websites.
- **22 retrieved records** and **118 verbatim evidence quotes** across 54 relationships.
- **One sourced next research question:** how the RTT Clinical Severity Scale and the CDD-specific severity assessment compare in people with CDD. The 2020 Natural History Study authors themselves suggest this comparison.

## What would need to be measured

To turn "10×" from a hypothesis into a result, run a small comparison: the same question, answered with and without RarePath, by patient-organization staff and by a research analyst. Measure:

- analyst hours to reach the same shortlist of reusable assets
- number of databases searched manually
- time to identify a relevant shared research asset
- time to assemble an evidence pack for a first collaboration conversation
- expert-rated accuracy and completeness of the connections and limitations surfaced, including false positives

The result counts only if RarePath's shortlist is at least as accurate as the manual one.

## Why this milestone matters

Shared infrastructure is a real precedent. Demarest et al. (2019) report that the CDD severity assessment was developed from the experience of the IFCR Centers of Excellence consortium and the NIH Rett and Rett-Related Disorders Natural History Study consortium. Making that kind of precedent findable early is how a small community avoids rebuilding what an adjacent one already has.
